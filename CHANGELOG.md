# Changelog

Changes that affect how books come out or how phoenix-ebook is used. Existing command lines keep working; where the output changed, the entry says how to get the old behavior if there is one.

## Unreleased

### Added
- **Defector, and WordPress blogs through their private full-text feed** (#49, ADR 0005): `--platform wordpress-feed --url https://defector.com`, with the blog's private feed address stored as its key (`--set-secret`). Each post is fetched from the feed by its slug; the website takes `defector.com/<slug>` addresses. Every co-author is credited (names only), dates are shown in the blog's time zone (`timezone` in its rules file), and the lead image goes above the post (no caption; reported as missing alt text). WordPress blocks become canonical HTML: embeds (YouTube, X, Bluesky, Instagram, …) and the blog's own videos become link cards, galleries become their images, pullquotes become quotations, and emoji pictures become emoji. Defector's podcasts and crosswords are left out of books (`skip_categories`) and reported; a book of only those stops with an error. Only the shared cleanup applies to Defector. New warnings: `post-left-out`, `dates-in-utc` (a WordPress blog with no time zone set) and `markup-unknown` (a block the platform doesn't know, kept as plain text, once per kind per book). Instagram embeds on any platform now become a "Post on Instagram" card.
- **One store for the website's blogs, keys and users** (#31, ADR 0004): with `PHOENIX_STORE_URL` set, the website reads blogs, their keys (encrypted with AES-256-GCM under `PHOENIX_STORE_MASTER_KEY`), users and access from a Postgres database (Neon's free plan), through a read-only login, on every request, so changes need no redeploy. If the database can't be reached, pages say to try again in a minute instead of signing anyone out. `scripts/store_admin.py import` loads it once from the user list, the stored keys and `BLOGS`; `export` and `import --from-export` back it up and restore it. Without the variable the website uses `PHOENIX_USERS`, `~/.phoenix_users.json` and the usual key lookup, as before. The command line is unchanged.
- **Default cleanup rules for every blog, and a plain rules file per blog** (#43): lines that only sell ("Subscribe now", "Get 20% off for 1 year", "Subscribe to read the rest"), paragraphs that are only a sign-up link, and share buttons are now removed from every book, and listed in the build report. What a blog removes on top of that is data, in `phoenix_ebook/sites/<name>.toml`, with plain phrases instead of code, and a blog can be only a file. Flaming Hydra books lose two more lines this way ("Subscribe! to Support Our Serpent" in `what-was-the-top-40`, "SUBSCRIBE TO THE MAGAZINE WORLD OF FLAMING HYDRA" in `issue-458`); use `keep_lines` to keep one. Welcome to Hell World books don't change.
- **Welcome to Hell World** (#38): books can be built from this second Ghost blog, on the command line (chosen automatically from `--url https://hellworld.ghost.io`) and on the website. Every button (each sells a subscription or a book) and the lines that only sell ("Subscribe now", "Get 20% off for 1 year", "Subscribe to read the rest") are removed and listed in the build report. A "read the rest" line stays when it links elsewhere and little follows it, because that post really is shortened. Sentences asking readers to subscribe, "Read the rest here" links, book notices and guest bylines stay.
- **Signing in to the website** (#34, ADR 0003): every page now needs a signed-in user: an email address and a password, kept as an argon2id hash in `PHOENIX_USERS` (on Vercel) or `~/.phoenix_users.json`. `scripts/add_user.py` adds users, resets passwords and removes users, printing the value to paste into Vercel. Sign-ins last 30 days (`PHOENIX_SESSION_SECRET` signs them); every page shows who's signed in, with a Sign out button. Forms sent from other sites are refused. Each build and each failed sign-in is logged. Running the website locally now needs a user too: run `scripts/add_user.py` once.
- **Kickstarter and Phoenix promos are removed from Flaming Hydra books** (#39): a post's last section, after its final rule, is left out when it promotes the blog's campaigns (a link to its Kickstarter projects or The Phoenix's sign-up form, or a heading naming them), and it's listed in the build report. Mentions elsewhere in a post stay.
- **Closing appeals are removed from Flaming Hydra books** (#30): a post's last section, after its final rule, is left out when it asks readers to subscribe or donate in plain text ("If you enjoyed this free post, subscribe or donate to Flaming Hydra…"), and each removal is listed after the build. Sections over 150 words are kept. Donate links now count as calls-to-action everywhere, so one inside a sentence loses its link (the words stay) and is reported, like a subscribe link. Some digest issues' closing promotions (e.g. the print-magazine notices) are removed by the same rule.
- **Content files in Word, Markdown or plain text** (#26): every content file (`--copyright-file`, `--foreword-file`, …, and the website's uploads) can now be a `.docx`, `.md` or `.txt` file as well as HTML, chosen by the extension. Word and Markdown keep headings, italics, bold, links and lists, with headings moved down a level so "# Foreword" or Word's Heading 1 becomes the page's heading; a converted page without a heading gets the section's name. Pictures in Word files are left out for now and reported (`content-image-left-out`). Files with other extensions are read as before.
- **A website for building books** (#23, #24, #25, #26; local for now, see the README's *Website* section): a form with a hint on every field. Every check runs before anything is built, and problems come back to the form. The build shows live progress and ends on a results page with the download link and every warning, grouped by post and explained in the troubleshooting guide's words. Images with missing or unhelpful alt text get a box and a thumbnail there, and one button rebuilds the book with the descriptions. For library use, `fetch_posts()` and `build()` take an optional `progress` callback. Alt-text warnings now carry the image's current alt text (`BuildProblem.alt`) and whether the image is all there is of a link (`BuildProblem.is_link`). Platforms can supply a small preview of an image (`Platform.thumbnail_url()`; Ghost uses its own resized WebP copies).

### Changed
- **Website books are deleted after 3 hours** (#33): download links, and the uploads kept for a rebuild, last 3 hours from the build, and the results page says so. A Vercel Cron job deletes older books every 15 minutes (it needs `CRON_SECRET`). A rebuild after that still works but asks for the cover and content files again. Books built before this change are deleted on the job's first run.
- **Users, blogs and keys are managed with commands** (#40): `scripts/store_admin.py user add | new-password | remove`, `blog add | set-key | short-name | list` and `new-login-passwords` change the store directly, with no paste into Vercel and no redeploy, and back it up after each change. `import` now only loads an empty store, because a later run would remove users and blogs added since; `import --from-export` still restores a backup. Its `--new-login-passwords` option is now the `new-login-passwords` command. Each blog has a short name for these commands (`hellworld`), stored in the store and chosen with `--short-name`.
- **Each website user builds only from their own blogs** (#41): with the store connected, the form names only the blogs a user has been given, addresses from another blog are refused before anything is fetched, and a user with none sees a message instead of the form. `scripts/store_admin.py grant`, `revoke` and `users` choose who builds from which blog; re-running the import keeps each user's blogs, and new users start with none. Without the store, everyone may still use every blog.
- **The website takes full post addresses only** (#38): it works out the blog from them, so bare slugs are no longer accepted, and a list with addresses from two blogs is an error. The command line still takes slugs.
- **Tweets with no text show who, when and a link** (#38): embeds imported from Substack hold only an empty link, and came out as a box saying just "Post on X". They now show the writer's handle, the date and a link to the tweet.
- **Long lists of alt-text boxes are folded** (#38): when a book has more than 20 images needing alt text, the website's results page folds each post's boxes under a count. Other warnings stay visible, and every image can still be fixed.
- **Links split into one link per word are joined back together** (#36): some posts store a linked phrase as one link per word plus one per space between them (23 in "Issue 655"). Before, a space link inside bold stayed in the book as an empty link (flagged by Ace), and a plain one was removed along with its space, so "with Greenland" became "withGreenland" and each one was reported as a removed link. Now neighbouring links to the same address become one link, and this isn't reported. A link with no words or image is removed even when its space is inside bold or italics (`<a><em> </em></a>`, flagged by Ace before), and it keeps its space. Across Flaming Hydra's 1,818 published posts: 35 posts had split links, 4 had words run together, and the build reports 106 fewer removed links.
- **More invitations to the comments are removed from Flaming Hydra books**: the closing "… in the Comments Section" line is now left out when no rule comes before it, and when it sits above the share buttons rather than at the very end (93 more posts, e.g. "The Insult That Made a Man Make Another Man"). A short line after a rule is still removed even if only part of it is a link; a sentence elsewhere that links to the comments is kept. Rules and empty paragraphs left at the end of a post are removed too.
- **Broken links in posts are repaired or unlinked, and every change is reported** (#22): in-post links to sections that don't exist (common in digest contents lists) are pointed at the section they meant, matched by its id or heading; addresses with stray spaces or quotes, `mailto: x`, a missing `https://` or `:`, or characters a link can't contain are fixed; links whose target can't be found, or whose "address" is plain text, lose the link but keep their words; links with no text are removed. Each one is listed after the build with its old and new address, so a wrong repair can be caught.
- **Flaming Hydra byline headings** (#22): "*by* Name" headings under section titles are now byline paragraphs, centred in small caps like the chapter header's byline, so they no longer count as headings in reading apps' navigation. One at the top of a post that repeats the chapter header's byline is removed.
- **Embedded media become link cards** (#21): YouTube, Vimeo and Spotify embeds, Ghost audio and video players, bookmark cards, and embedded posts from X, TikTok and Bluesky each become a small card in the text: a label ("Video", "Audio", "Link", "Post on X", …), the linked title or the post's text, a source line ("YouTube", "Tim Adams · The Guardian", "Name (@handle) · 23 Apr 2025"), and, for videos and bookmarks, a thumbnail. Before, embeds vanished and left an empty figure (184 Flaming Hydra articles), embedded posts were centred with raw timestamps, and bookmark cards showed the site's small icon at full width. Thumbnails are decorative (not reported as missing alt text). TikTok, Vimeo and Spotify thumbnails are looked up when the book is built; if that fails, the card stays without one and the build reports it. Forms and polls (Tally) are left out and reported; anything unrecognised becomes a generic "Embedded content" card and is reported.
- **Website calls-to-action removed from books** (#20): trailing subscribe banners (with their heading and caption), and subscribe/support/share-on-Bluesky/shop buttons, are removed; subscribe and Ghost "portal" links inside sentences keep their words but lose the link; each such paragraph and any mid-post promotional image (kept, since it may be the post's content) is reported after the build. Removed banners are never downloaded, so books no longer declare a flashing hazard just because of the animated logo, and image and alt-text counts change accordingly.
- **Friendly errors** (#14): common mistakes print one actionable line instead of a Python traceback, with exit code 1: a wrong or malformed key, no stored key, mistyped slugs (all listed at once), the public site URL instead of the Ghost address (it redirects, which drops the key), an unreachable or firewall-blocked host (with the firewall's explanation), a broken or incomplete manifest, an unknown platform or processor, a missing output folder, and a content file that isn't UTF-8. `--debug` shows the traceback; real bugs always do. Ctrl-C prints `interrupted` (exit 130).
- **No half-written books** (#14): the EPUB is written to a temporary file and renamed into place when complete, so a failed or interrupted build never leaves a broken book or damages an existing one.
- **Credits** (#5): `--author` is now `--editor` (the old flag still works). The editor is credited as the book's creator, and each post's author as a contributor with a sort name ("Connor, J.D."), so reading apps don't shelve an anthology under one writer. Without an editor, post authors are the creators. For library use, `BookSpec.author` is renamed `BookSpec.editor`.
- **No default rights statement** (#5): the hard-coded "© All rights reserved." is gone; pass `--rights` to include one.
- **Identifier format** (#5): ISBNs are stored as `urn:isbn:…` (typed as ISBN-13 or ISBN-10); books without one get `urn:uuid:…`.
- **New look** (#7): books now use Standard Ebooks' `core.css` plus phoenix-ebook's own stylesheet: centered title page, small-caps dates and bylines, book-style paragraphs, figures kept on one screen with their captions, and a "Contents" page without numbering. The previous stylesheet forced the Georgia font; now no font is set, so readers' own font settings apply. Tall images are capped to the screen height instead of overflowing.
- **Book structure** (#6): a generated **title page** now opens the book, followed by copyright, imprint, the **contents page** (moved here from page 2), foreword and introduction; a **half-title page** divides front matter from the chapters. The contents page starts at the foreword: it no longer lists the title page, copyright, imprint, the half-title page or itself.
- **No cover page** (#6): the cover is declared as the book's cover image only, as Kindle's guidelines recommend; reading apps show it in the library. The book opens on the title page.
- **Chapter titles are `<h2>`** (#6), under the title page's `<h1>`; generated placeholder headings are `<h2>` too. If your content files start with `<h1>`, change them to `<h2>`.
- **Chapter headers** (#4): each chapter now opens with the publication date (in the site's timezone), the title, and a "By …" byline linking to each author's page. Contents entries are plain titles by default; Flaming Hydra books keep "Title — Author" (the site's processor decides).
- **Feature images** (#2): each post's lead image, with its alt text and caption, now appears at the top of its chapter. Previously it was missing.
- **Cleaner chapter markup** (#3): Ghost editor markup (`kg-*` classes, inline styles, `<!--members-only-->` comments, stray `<br>`, nested bold) is removed. Visible text is unchanged.
- **Image optimization on by default**: post images are scaled to at most 1100px wide and re-encoded as JPEG (quality 85); a 10-post test book went from 17.6 MB to 4.6 MB. Old behavior: `--keep-original-images`. Tune with `--image-max-width` and `--image-quality`, or an `[images]` table in the manifest.
- **The Python package is now `phoenix_ebook`** (was `phoenix`), since phoenix-ebook is one module of the larger Phoenix project. Secret storage names are unchanged and shared with other Phoenix modules.

### Fixed
- **Rebuilding a book with hundreds of alt-text boxes** (#38) no longer hits the form parser's limit of 1,000 fields.
- **Plain-text content broke the book** (#26): an `&` or `<` in a plain-text introduction ("Arts & Letters") made an invalid page; text is now escaped.
- **Invalid books from post markup** (#22): empty headings (Flaming Hydra posts often have an empty `<h4>`) and empty `id`s are removed, and old-style `<a name>` link targets become ids, so books built from these posts pass epubcheck. On the Flaming Hydra test sample, epubcheck went from 13 errors to none and DAISY Ace from 47 findings to 2 (both linked images without alt text, already in the alt-text report).
- **Audio and video players made invalid books** (#21): Ghost's players (icons, sliders, remote media) failed epubcheck; they are now link cards.
- **Images stored under the wrong type** (#21): an image server that sends a PNG or WebP from a `.jpg` address produced a file epubcheck rejects; images are now stored by what they are.
- **Bogus page list** (regression from #9): every chapter was listed as a printed "page" (labelled "article-2", "article-3", …), which Thorium uses for page numbers and "go to page". Books no longer include a page list (they have no print page numbers). Found by DAISY Ace.
- **A content-file path that doesn't exist** (e.g. a typo in `--foreword-file`) now stops the build with a clear error before anything is fetched; it used to produce a placeholder page silently (or crash, for the cover and intro).
- **Images that can't be downloaded** (#1) are left out and listed as warnings after the build, instead of leaving a remote link in the book (which made the EPUB invalid). HTTP error pages are no longer embedded as images. Temporary failures are retried once.
- **Cover reference** was placed in the wrong part of the package file, which failed epubcheck; books now validate cleanly.
- **Titles containing `&` or `<`** no longer produce an invalid EPUB.

### Added
- **Troubleshooting guide** (#15): [docs/troubleshooting.md](docs/troubleshooting.md) explains every message a build can print (errors that stop it, warnings after it), what each means and what to do, plus exit codes, `--debug`, and the hosts a build contacts (for firewalls). The build report now links to it when there are warnings. A test keeps it complete: a new warning or error without an entry fails the suite.
- **Footnotes become proper notes** (#18): Ghost Markdown-card footnotes ("[1]" markers and a list at the end of the post) are now marked as notes: markers show as plain superscript numbers with a larger tap area, the list gets a "Notes" heading, and Thorium opens a note in a pop-up when its number is tapped. Notes stay at the end of their chapter, with "↩︎" links back.
- For library use, `build()` accepts an optional `platform`, used for platform-specific image-URL rules when matching `[alt_text]` overrides (#19). Internally, each platform now converts its HTML to one canonical form before processing (ADR 0001); books are unchanged.
- **Accessibility metadata** (#8): access modes, features, hazards and a summary, computed from each book's content (e.g. "text descriptions for images" is claimed only when every image has alt text), plus ARIA roles for chapters, foreword, introduction and acknowledgments, and the book language on the package. No WCAG conformance claim. DAISY Ace now passes on the test book (it reported 29 findings before). Chapters are wrapped in `<section>` instead of `<article>` (required for the chapter ARIA role).
- **Section roles and better line breaks** (#9): every page now declares its part of the book (front matter, body matter, back matter) and, where it fits, what it is (copyright page, imprint, foreword, introduction, chapter, acknowledgments), for reading apps' navigation. (Screen readers mostly rely on matching ARIA roles, which aren't added yet; see #8.) An invisible no-break character before em dashes and ellipses stops lines from starting with "—" or "…" (on a 360px-wide screen, 6 of 106 did before, none now). Your content files are wrapped, never changed.
- **Alt text report and overrides** (#10): after each build, images with missing or unhelpful alt text are listed (post, position, URL, caption) with a ready-to-paste manifest block. A manifest `[alt_text]` table supplies or replaces alt text by image URL; an empty value marks an image decorative.
- `--subtitle`, `--series`, `--series-number`, `--issn`, `--rights` and the manifest's `[sort_names]` table (#5). The ISSN identifies the series and is shown with it on the title page.
- ISBN and ISSN validation (#5): an invalid number (wrong length or check digit) stops the build before anything is fetched. Subtitle and series appear on the title page.
- `--css` (manifest: `[style] css`): add your own stylesheet, applied after the built-in ones.
- `--no-placeholders` (manifest: `[content] placeholders = false`): leave out sections that have no file instead of adding placeholder pages.
- EPUB landmarks for the title page, contents page and start of the text, which some reading apps use to decide where a book opens.
- `--image-max-width`, `--image-quality`, `--keep-original-images` and the manifest `[images]` table.
- For library use, `build()` now returns `BuildResult(path, problems)` instead of a bare path.
- An offline test suite (`.venv/bin/python -m pytest`), including epubcheck validation.
